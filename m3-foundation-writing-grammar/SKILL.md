# English UI grammar and punctuation

Source: [Grammar and punctuation](https://m3.material.io/foundations/content-design/style-guide/grammar-and-punctuation).

This guidance applies to English-language UX writing. Material generally follows AP Style.

## Sentences and emphasis

- Omit unnecessary punctuation and periods on single sentences, especially labels, tooltips, bullets, dialog body text, and links.
- Use periods for multiple sentences and for long or complex sentences when the context warrants them.
- Use contractions to improve comprehension and scanning. An uncontracted negative can add emphasis when caution is needed.
- Use exclamation points sparingly, such as for greetings or congratulations.
- Avoid exclamation points for routine tasks and empty states.
- Use bold rather than italics for general emphasis.
- Limit italics to a word or phrase, such as a name or title. Do not italicize whole sentences.
- Avoid blocks of capital letters. Use sentence case for product text.

## Lists, numbers, and symbols

- Use serial commas, except before an ampersand.
- Place commas inside quotation marks.
- Use commas for numbers between 1,000 and 1 million.
- Do not add grouping commas to identifiers such as addresses, radio frequencies, or years.
- Above 1 million, let context determine comma use. Rounded values with “M” can convey volume instead of exact counts.
- Omit colons in list headings. Use a colon to introduce a list within body text.
- Spell out “and” in paragraphs, sentences, email subjects, and before the final item in lists of three or more.
- Ampersands can fit short headings, headers, navigation labels, and buttons when space is limited.
- Prefer “and” when space permits.
- Use parentheses to define acronyms or jargon, or to reference a source.
- Do not use parentheses for side notes. Include needed information in the sentence.

## Ellipses and dashes

- Use ellipses sparingly for progress or incomplete text.
- Do not put a space before an ellipsis.
- Do not add ellipses to buttons or menu items that open a dialog or start a process.
- Coordinate truncation with engineering because ellipses may be added automatically.
- Avoid em dashes when a comma, period, or new sentence can simplify the text.
- Use an en dash without spaces for a range.
- Use hyphens without surrounding spaces for closely related words and negative numbers.
- Hyphenate adjective phrases when needed for clarity. Common phrases and proper nouns may not need hyphens.
- Hyphenate noun phrases where appropriate, including a verb plus adverb acting as a noun.
- Do not hyphenate a verb phrase consisting of a verb and an adverb or preposition.

## Review

Check punctuation against the text's role and context, rather than imposing the same punctuation on every UI string.
