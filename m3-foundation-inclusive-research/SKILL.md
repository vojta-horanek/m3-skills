# User needs and co-design

## User needs

Source: [User needs](https://m3.material.io/foundations/building-for-all/user-needs).

- Work with communities, organizations, experts, and researchers to understand experiences the team does not represent.
- Include perspectives from people who are often overlooked.
- Consider attributes and environments in research, design, comprehensive testing, and marketing.
- Consider age, culture, disability, education, literacy, ethnicity, gender, geography, and global relevance.
- Also consider physical attributes, race, religion, sexual orientation, socioeconomic status, and technology proficiency.
- Treat these dimensions as an evolving list, rather than a complete account of human experience.

## Co-design

Source: [Co-design](https://m3.material.io/foundations/building-for-all/co-design).

- Involve people who use the product in its creation. Co-design extends beyond traditional user research.
- Engage people with varied backgrounds and experiences throughout development.
- Validate assumptions through market research with a broad range of people.
- Ask who else could benefit and what those people need.
- Include overlooked communities in ongoing testing.
- Engage community members and experts early and often.
- Use their feedback to shape feature priorities, functionality, and messaging.
- Carry insights beyond ideation into later development stages.
- Consider the risks and harms of exclusion and how to address them.
- For tools that use ML or AI, consider how to reduce bias.

## Review participation

Check who contributed, whose needs remain unrepresented, and how feedback affected decisions.
Check whether participation continues through testing and later development stages.
