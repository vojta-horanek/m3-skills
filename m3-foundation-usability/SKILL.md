# Usability and M3 Expressive

## Goals and evaluation

Source: [Usability overview](https://m3.material.io/foundations/usability/overview).

Evaluate how effectively users interact with the product through these aspects:

| Aspect | Evaluation focus |
| --- | --- |
| Efficiency | Complete tasks and goals efficiently |
| Errors | Reduce mistakes and make correction easy |
| Learnability | Let first-time users learn and complete tasks |
| Memorability | Let returning users remember how to use the product |
| Satisfaction | Support satisfaction with the experience |

- Distinguish usability from accessibility. Accessibility supports perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust experiences for people with disabilities.
- Identify primary, secondary, and tertiary goals from product needs and user satisfaction.
- Give primary goals the strongest emphasis while keeping supporting goals discoverable.
- Consider one primary task per page and empty space to focus attention.
- Make frequent core actions recognizable and reachable.
- Test and gather feedback early and often across users and contexts. Iterate to reduce errors and validate choices.

## Design tactics

- Establish hierarchy through color, contrast, containment, size, spacing, placement, and typography.
- Use color contrast that meets accessibility guidance.
- Group related content through subtle containers, spacing, and headings.
- Use motion sparingly to emphasize key moments. Motion can distract.
- Use shape to guide focus, distinguish controls or containers, and signal interaction.
- Shape morph can communicate interaction states and progress.
- Give the most important action the largest scale and strongest emphasis.
- Use typography to distinguish primary, secondary, and supporting information.
- Group similar content through the same type style.
- Do not combine too many expressive tactics at once.

## Lessons from the Material example

Source: [Applying M3 Expressive](https://m3.material.io/foundations/usability/applying-m-3-expressive).

The Aura example demonstrates choices for different goals. Its specific colors, flower shapes, and breathing behavior are example details.

- Use scale, placement, color, and containment to make a primary action easy to find.
- Keep supporting settings grouped and less prominent than the main task.
- In a focused experience, emphasize the content guiding the task and keep secondary controls reachable.
- Reduce expressive emphasis on secondary and tertiary views to limit cognitive load.
- Use consistent color roles to distinguish actions and data.
- Avoid many equally large elements that compete for attention.
- Use uniform shapes, balanced sizes, clear spacing, and consistent alignment to support comparison.
- Avoid overlapping data containers and simultaneous competing motion.
- Keep text spacing stable when another animated element guides the task.
- Test refinements with users. The example improves by grouping settings and emphasizing the primary action.

## Verify usability

Check whether users identify the next action, complete the primary task, and recover from errors.
Check whether hierarchy reduces distraction while supporting learnability, memorability, and satisfaction.
