# UI writing

## Content direction

Source: [Content design overview](https://m3.material.io/foundations/content-design/overview).

Make UI text understandable to anyone. Material follows AP Style unless its guidance specifies otherwise.

## Clear and scannable copy

Source: [UX writing best practices](https://m3.material.io/foundations/content-design/style-guide/ux-writing-best-practices).

- State an action's consequences in neutral, direct language.
- Tell users what will happen and how they can undo the action when that applies.
- Do not misrepresent consequences or steer the decision through alarming or condescending wording.
- Use specific titles, headings, and subheadings to group information and support scanning.
- Use sentence case for titles, headings, labels, menus, navigation, app bars, and buttons unless specified otherwise.
- Preserve capitalization for products and branded terms.
- Spell out words where possible. Abbreviations can hinder comprehension and screen readers.
- Avoid Latin abbreviations in UI text. Use full phrases instead.
- When an abbreviation is appropriate, use its correct spelling and format.

## Pronouns

Source: [Style guide word choice](https://m3.material.io/foundations/content-design/style-guide/word-choice).

- Prefer the user's perspective through “you” and “your.”
- Do not mix first-person and second-person forms in the same context.
- Consider removing a pronoun when it does not help.
- Use caution with “I,” “we,” and “our” when speaking for a large company.
- For the company voice, prefer user-centered wording without first-person pronouns.
- First person can suit legal agreements or acknowledgments when it emphasizes the user's ownership of a consequential decision.

## Review

Check consequences, information grouping, capitalization, abbreviations, and consistency of perspective.
